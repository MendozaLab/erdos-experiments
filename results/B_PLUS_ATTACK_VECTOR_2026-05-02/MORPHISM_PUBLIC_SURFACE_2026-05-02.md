# Morphism Public Surface - 2026-05-02

## Public-Safe Definition

A morphism is a putative structural correspondence between two mathematical problem neighborhoods. It is not a discovered fact and not a proof. It becomes useful only when it predicts transferable structure that survives checks outside the example that suggested it.

Public sentence:

> A putative morphism is a structural correspondence tested by triangulated evidence: literature support, a target result, transfer to a different problem, a collider or failure test, and a formal checkpoint when available.

## Validation Ladder

1. Literature support: the proposed correspondence does not contradict the known problem statement, best-known results, or standard taxonomy.
2. Target result: the method produces a bounded finite result, certificate, or formal subclaim on the target problem.
3. Cross-problem transfer: the same pattern works on a second problem without retuning, or it fails in a way that explains the boundary.
4. Collider/failure test: finite experiments include at least one demoted or negative-control example, not only success cases.
5. Formal checkpoint: surviving finite claims route to Lean or another explicit proof/certificate layer.

## Safe To Disclose

- The word "putative" and the ladder above.
- Individual finite result packets and their SHA-verified manifests.
- Pass/fail/demoted Leg-4 outcomes.
- The claim that the Collider is a proof-target generator, not a theorem prover.
- The evidence ceiling: finite laboratory evidence, not theorem resolution.

## Quarantine Until Filing Or Explicit Approval

## Public-Safe Boundary For This Packet

The public framework can say that ErdosAtlas maps, checks, demotes, and routes putative structural correspondences. It cannot say that the atlas discovers true morphisms, proves Erdos problems, solves #30, or matches autonomous theorem solvers on theorem resolution.

The safe differentiator is not "we solve like Aristotle." It is "we maintain an auditable map-check-formalize instrument with a falsification layer."
