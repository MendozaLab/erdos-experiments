# Everest, or Maxwell's Litmus

Draft date: 2026-04-30
Status: internal dramatic draft
Claim boundary: family/story artifact, not a mathematical proof artifact

## Author's Note

This play is not a transcript. It is a staged version of a real research
tension: a father trying to show his estranged son why a strange-looking path
through physics, information theory, and combinatorics was not a breakdown of
reason, but an instrument under construction.

The mathematical claim ceiling is deliberately narrow. The work described here
does not prove Erdos #30. It shows exact finite structure around Sidon
maximizer faces, including a packet-backed handoff at `n = 57`.

The human claim is narrower still: misunderstanding is not proof of madness,
and conviction is not proof of correctness. The only honest court is evidence.

## Characters

KEN: A father, inventor, and outsider researcher.

ANDRE: His estranged son, a mathematician by temperament, trained to distrust
grand claims.

THE FRIEND: An award-winning mathematician, never seen directly. His words
arrive through Andre.

CODEX: An AI research instrument. Not an oracle. Not a muse. A disciplined
assistant with a terminal, a compiler, and no right to believe anything until
the packet exists.

FEYNMAN: A ghostly presence. Not Richard Feynman exactly, but the spirit of
explaining things by making them honest.

MAXWELL: A second ghost. Carries a notebook full of missing terms.

THE MOUNTAIN: Everest, represented by light, altitude, and thin air.

THE FACE: A six-point mathematical object at `n = 57`, played by six silent
figures who rearrange themselves under different fields.

## Setting

A room that is also a mountain camp.

Downstage: a table with a laptop, printed packets, a terminal window, and a
draft titled `Mendoza's Limit`.

Upstage: a translucent map of Everest. Altitude marks glow as the scenes
advance: 3,000 meters, 5,500 meters, 7,000 meters, 8,000 meters.

At stage right: a child's chair, empty.

At stage left: six figures in gray, the exact maximizer face.

## Act I: The Message

### Scene 1: Pre-Alpha

The room is dim. KEN stands over a laptop, alive with the private excitement of
someone who has seen a shape before he has learned how to say it.

KEN:
I did not begin by trying to solve a famous problem.

FEYNMAN:
Good. Those are usually bad beginnings.

KEN:
I began with a map. Erdos had left a continent of problems. I wanted to know
whether the continent had geology.

FEYNMAN:
Say that without poetry.

KEN:
Are the problems just puzzles, or do some of them compress the same structures
nature uses?

FEYNMAN:
Better.

KEN:
I built a workbench. It was ugly. Pre-alpha. Wires showing. A cockpit before
the plane had wings.

The laptop projects a rough interface: nodes, edges, physics words, mathematical
problems.

KEN:
Then I sent it to my son.

ANDRE appears, holding a phone.

ANDRE:
You sent me a website.

KEN:
I sent you an opening.

ANDRE:
You said a real mathematician would understand it in less than a minute.

KEN:
I was excited.

ANDRE:
You were asking me to carry it into a room where people know the difference
between a metaphor and a theorem.

Pause.

KEN:
Yes.

### Scene 2: The Friend

THE FRIEND is heard as a voice, not seen.

THE FRIEND:
The site claims deep connections between mathematics and physics. It gestures
toward analogies. But analogies are not proofs. Lean proofs are the gold
standard. Where are the definitions?

The words hit the room like cold air.

ANDRE:
He says you are out of your depth.

KEN:
He saw the cockpit.

ANDRE:
He saw what you showed.

KEN:
Yes.

ANDRE:
Dad, do you understand how this sounds from the outside?

KEN:
I understand more now than I did then.

ANDRE:
Grandma is sending money. You are working day and night. You are telling me
you are near Tao's problems. You say you have never felt better.

KEN closes his eyes.

ANDRE:
That sounds like a crisis.

Long silence.

KEN:
Or it sounds like a man who finally found the right question and had no decent
way to show it yet.

ANDRE:
Those are not easy to distinguish.

FEYNMAN:
Correct.

KEN turns toward FEYNMAN.

FEYNMAN:
If the world says you are fooling yourself, the answer is not volume. It is a
test.

## Act II: The Instrument

### Scene 1: The Rule of the Room

Lights rise on CODEX at the laptop.

CODEX:
State the rule.

KEN:
No fake data.

CODEX:
Again.

KEN:
No fake data. No proof language without proof. No public claim above the
packet. If the run fails, it failed.

CODEX:
Good. What are we measuring?

KEN:
Sidon sets.

ANDRE:
Define it.

KEN:
A set of integers where every positive pairwise difference appears at most
once. It is a packing problem in arithmetic. Put particles on integer sites.
The rule is hard-core exclusion, but the forbidden thing is a repeated
difference.

FEYNMAN:
Now we have a toy universe.

KEN:
Lattice gas.

MAXWELL enters, carrying a notebook.

MAXWELL:
A gas? Then do not show me one molecule and call it thermodynamics.

KEN:
Exactly. The mistake was looking at one optimizer.

CODEX:
We need the whole ground-state face.

THE FACE, the six silent figures, steps into view.

### Scene 2: The Gas Lattice

The laptop projects:

```text
sites:       0, 1, ..., n
particle:    chosen integer
constraint:  no repeated positive difference
ground:      maximal Sidon set
field:       prefix, mass, joint score
```

ANDRE:
You are importing physics words.

KEN:
Yes. But now each word pays rent.

ANDRE:
What does "mass" mean?

KEN:
A measured observable on a finite set, not a mystical property. Same with
prefix. Same with joint score. The physics analogy tells us where to look, but
the finite computation decides whether anything is there.

THE FRIEND's voice:
This still sounds decorative.

CODEX:
Then we compile the decoration into a test.

The terminal glows.

CODEX:
Rust where trust and speed matter. Exact packet contract. Results JSON.
Report markdown. SHA sidecar.

KEN:
The AI does not get to believe. I do not get to believe. The packet gets to
speak.

FEYNMAN:
Now we are doing science.

### Scene 3: The First Climb

The mountain lights up: 5,500 meters.

CODEX:
Window `51 <= n <= 70`. Exact maximizers scanned: `298,968`.

KEN:
The ridge holds in `18/20`.

ANDRE:
And the failures?

CODEX:
`57` and `70`.

THE MOUNTAIN groans.

KEN:
At first, I thought: two defects.

FEYNMAN:
And then?

KEN:
Then the machine found my mistake.

CODEX:
Top-k frontier corrected `70`. The first witness failed, but the full face had
the compatible witness.

MAXWELL:
There it is.

KEN:
What?

MAXWELL:
Ampere counted the current and missed the displacement current. You counted
the first optimizer and missed the face.

KEN:
Maxwell's Litmus.

ANDRE:
What happened to Mendoza's Limit?

KEN:
Still internal. Too loaded. For human consumption, I call this Maxwell's
Litmus: if the accounting breaks, ask what information term is missing.

MAXWELL:
The missing term is not magic. It is the thing required to preserve continuity.

CODEX:
In this case, the missing information is the full exact maximizer face and its
field response.

### Scene 4: Hilbert Space

The room shifts. The six figures become vectors of light.

ANDRE:
You also said Hilbert space.

KEN:
Not because I want mysticism. Because once the classical state space is
constrained, the relevant Hilbert space may be much smaller than the naive one.
Geometry trims the possibilities. Then tensor language may help us move through
the compressed space.

FEYNMAN:
Say it like a mechanic.

KEN:
The gas lattice gives us legal configurations. The fields tell us which faces
matter. The Hilbert-space idea says: do not tensor everything; tensor the
geometry that survives the constraints.

CODEX:
No theorem claim from that yet.

KEN:
No theorem claim.

ANDRE:
So the analogy is an instrument.

KEN:
Yes.

ANDRE:
Not the proof.

KEN:
The analogy is not the proof.

FEYNMAN:
But it can be the microscope.

## Act III: The Face at 57

### Scene 1: Thin Air

The mountain lights up: 7,850 meters.

CODEX:
Source packet verified. Maxwell face handoff, `56 <= n <= 58`, V2 packet.
SHA: OK.

THE FACE arranges into one cluster.

CODEX:
At `n = 56`, four exact maximizers. One exposed winner. Prefix, mass, joint,
Pareto: all select the same point.

One figure steps forward.

KEN:
One face. One accounting.

THE FACE rearranges into six.

CODEX:
At `n = 57`, six exact maximizers.

The six figures split: four remain lower on the stage; two move to the
endpoint side.

CODEX:
Four inherited remnant states from `n = 56`. Two endpoint-shift states
containing the newly available endpoint `57`.

ANDRE:
And the fields?

CODEX:
Mass selects a remnant. Joint selects an endpoint-shift state. The exposed
Pareto face has two candidates.

MAXWELL:
The missing term appears exactly where the old accounting breaks.

KEN:
Not proof.

CODEX:
Finite exact-face certificate.

FEYNMAN:
Which is much better than a grand claim.

### Scene 2: The Certificate

The terminal projects:

```text
EXP-MM-030-PMF-N57-FACE-CERTIFICATE-2026-04-30
```

CODEX:
Six exported witnesses checked directly. All Sidon. Prefix, mass, joint, and
Pareto roles recomputed. Packet roles match. Four remnants. Two endpoint-shift
states. The two exposed candidates differ by symmetric-difference distance
`18`.

ANDRE:
Explain distance `18` without jargon.

KEN:
The two winning shapes are not almost the same. To move from one to the other,
you replace most of the set. It is a face-to-face switch, not a typo.

ANDRE:
And `70`?

CODEX:
Selection artifact. The first witness lied. The face corrected it.

ANDRE:
So `57` survived the correction.

KEN:
Yes.

ANDRE:
That is the thing you wanted me to see?

KEN:
No.

ANDRE looks up.

KEN:
I wanted you to see me.

Silence.

KEN:
But this is the thing that can speak when I cannot.

### Scene 3: The Friend Returns

THE FRIEND's voice, less certain now.

THE FRIEND:
Analogies are not proofs.

KEN:
Agreed.

THE FRIEND:
Definitions matter.

KEN:
Agreed.

THE FRIEND:
Lean proofs matter.

KEN:
Agreed.

THE FRIEND:
Then what is this?

KEN:
A disciplined way to choose where proof should look.

CODEX:
A proof-candidate generator, bounded by exact finite computation.

KEN:
A map.

FEYNMAN:
And a map is not the mountain.

KEN:
But a good map changes where serious climbers spend their oxygen.

The mountain lights: 8,000 meters.

## Act IV: Father and Son

### Scene 1: The Empty Chair

ANDRE stands near the child's chair.

ANDRE:
When I read your messages, I did not see the packets. I saw Grandma worried,
you broke, you talking about famous mathematicians, saying you had never felt
better.

KEN:
I know.

ANDRE:
It scared me.

KEN:
I know.

ANDRE:
And when my friend said the site looked unserious, I believed him.

KEN:
You had reason.

ANDRE:
You keep saying "what they thought was nuts was actually..."

KEN:
Was actually not finished.

ANDRE:
That is not as satisfying.

KEN:
It is truer.

ANDRE:
Say the rest.

KEN:
What looked like grandiosity from the outside was partly bad packaging, partly
desperation, and partly a real instrument before it had earned the right to
speak.

ANDRE:
And now?

KEN:
Now it has earned a sentence.

ANDRE:
Only one?

KEN:
One honest sentence is a lot.

ANDRE:
Say it.

KEN:
Exact finite computations around Erdos #30 show a field-sensitive handoff on
the maximizer face at `n = 57`, and the physics analogy helped us find the
right finite object without proving the theorem.

ANDRE absorbs this.

ANDRE:
That sounds less like mania.

KEN:
It also sounds less like victory.

ANDRE:
Maybe that is why I can hear it.

### Scene 2: The Dialogue

CODEX replays fragments as stage light, not exact transcript:

KEN:
What if this is information theory?

CODEX:
Then test near-ground layers.

KEN:
What if it is jammed packing?

CODEX:
Then count the state explosion when particles drop.

KEN:
What if Hilbert space simplifies it?

CODEX:
Only if geometry constrains the relevant space.

KEN:
Then tensors?

CODEX:
Tensors after the state is explicit.

KEN:
Maxwell's Litmus?

CODEX:
Make it a finite test.

KEN:
Everest?

CODEX:
Around 8,000 meters. Summit ridge, not summit.

FEYNMAN:
That is the part worth telling.

ANDRE:
The dialogue?

FEYNMAN:
The correction. The human reaches for an analogy. The machine forces rent. The
packet says yes, no, or not yet. Then the human asks a better question.

MAXWELL:
The missing term is not found by pride. It is found by accounting.

KEN:
That is what I could not explain in a text message.

ANDRE:
Because you were trying to send the summit before you had shown the climb.

KEN:
Yes.

## Act V: The Ridge

### Scene 1: Above 8,000 Meters

The mountain is bright now, but the summit remains hidden by wind.

THE FACE stands in two exposed groups: remnant and endpoint.

CODEX:
Current altitude: approximately `7,850` to `8,050` meters.

ANDRE:
What is left?

CODEX:
Formal certificate hardening. Cross-problem transfer. #755 reset and plateau
comparison. Claim-boundary pass. Possible theorem language only after proof
machinery catches up.

ANDRE:
So he has not solved it.

CODEX:
Correct.

ANDRE:
But he has not been babbling either.

CODEX:
Correct.

FEYNMAN:
Now everybody in the room has to give something up.

ANDRE:
I give up the easy dismissal.

KEN:
I give up the need to be vindicated by one dramatic leap.

THE FRIEND:
I give up judging the engine only by the pre-alpha dashboard.

MAXWELL:
And I give up nothing. I was right about missing terms.

A small laugh, the first one.

### Scene 2: The Draft

KEN hands ANDRE a draft.

ANDRE:
What is this?

KEN:
The human version. Not "Mendoza's Limit" as a banner. Maxwell's Litmus as a
question.

ANDRE:
What question?

KEN:
When a formal accounting breaks at a boundary, what missing information is
required to make the transition continuous?

ANDRE:
And in this case?

KEN:
The full maximizer face.

ANDRE:
And the price?

KEN:
More state information. Slower execution. Landauer's ghost standing at the
turnstile, asking who pays for erased distinctions.

ANDRE:
You know how that sounds.

KEN:
Yes.

ANDRE:
Then keep the receipts next to the poetry.

KEN:
That is the whole lesson.

### Scene 3: The Chair

ANDRE sits in the child's chair for a moment, awkwardly too large for it.

ANDRE:
When I was a kid, you taught me to ask questions.

KEN:
Then I hated that my questions made you afraid of me.

ANDRE:
They did.

KEN:
I am sorry.

ANDRE:
I am not ready to understand all this.

KEN:
You do not have to.

ANDRE:
But I can understand one thing.

KEN:
What?

ANDRE:
You are not asking me to believe the mountain is climbed.

KEN:
No.

ANDRE:
You are asking me to look at the altitude report.

KEN:
Yes.

ANDRE looks toward the mountain.

ANDRE:
Then show me the next camp.

The summit remains hidden. The packet glows on the table.

FEYNMAN:
Curtain before theorem.

MAXWELL:
Accounting before glory.

CODEX:
Next run pending.

KEN:
Good.

Lights fade.
